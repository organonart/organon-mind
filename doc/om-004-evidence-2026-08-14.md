# Evidence pass — organon-one, 2026-08-14

Labels used throughout: **VERIFIED** = measured today on this machine, method
stated. **TESTIMONY** = from the change log or a doc comment, not re-derived.

---

## 1. The corrector curve — VERIFIED, and it holds

Re-derived because 87/142/281 ms was a doc comment and is the load-bearing
number. n=7 per point, warm, real human speech cut from **60 s into**
`organon-read-final.wav` (the first 30 s is lead-in silence at −51.1 dB and
transcribes as "Hmm" — it has already invalidated one measurement here).
Server-reported `infer`, not wall clock, so network is excluded.

| clip | testimony | unbiased | **biased α2.0, 47 terms (deployed)** |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 2 s | 87 ms | 54 (52–67) | **70 (60–83)** |
| 10 s | 142 ms | 94 (85–111) | **104 (96–111)** |
| 30 s | 281 ms | 213 (202–234) | **245 (227–278)** |

**~6.3 ms per second of audio over a ~64 ms fixed floor.** 15× the audio costs
3.5× the time. The claim that fixed cost dominates is confirmed and the current
numbers are *faster* than the testimony.

**Warm only, deliberately.** "Cold" is not a point on this curve — it is a model
load (TESTIMONY: 8.5 s warm, 24 s cold, 51 s from a cold page cache), a different
phenomenon that happens once per service lifetime. In production the model is
resident, so every pass a user experiences is warm.

⚠️ **One anomaly, observed once and not reproduced.** A `bias_apply_ms` of
**8083 ms** on a cache miss (`bias_cache_hit: false`). Normal is 0–33 ms and it
is 0 once cached. It appeared while I was passing a malformed term list, so it
may be an artifact of my own error rather than a property of the server. Recorded
because an 8-second bias application would be a real production hazard if it can
happen with a valid list — but I will not claim it does.

⚠️ **A defect in my first run, disclosed because it changes how to read it.** I
built the bias list with `paste -sd,` over the raw file, so comments and blanks
became terms; the server reported `terms=2` where 47 were intended. The table
above is the corrected run. The first run's *unbiased* column was unaffected and
agreed (78/118/213).

## 2. Where revisions land — VERIFIED, and it **does not support the strong claim**

### The instrument had to be built

Neither existing surface can answer this:

- the tray log records **final text plus a refinement count**, no intermediate
  passes and no per-word history;
- `--wav --realtime` runs **the scribe only** — `transcriber.stream()`, no
  refiner.

So this reconstructs the corrector's own behaviour, which is where the claim
lives: feed it growing prefixes of one utterance at the real 1 Hz cadence and
diff consecutive passes with `difflib` opcodes. **Appends do not count** — only a
word already emitted and then changed. Three segments, 87 passes.

### Result

7 events were **excluded as a measurement artifact, not a finding**: clips are
cut at a fixed offset so they open mid-word, and the leading partial phoneme
oscillates in and out, each occurrence scoring a perfect 1.0 back. Counting them
would have inflated exactly the statistic under test. 39 revisions remain.

**How far back** (1.0 = first word, 0.0 = last written)

```
min 0.00   p25 0.00   median 0.13   p75 0.33   max 0.67
>25% back: 12/39 (31%)      >50% back: 4/39 (10%)
```

**How long that text had been standing**

```
min 1 s   median 2 s   p75 4 s   max 12 s
standing >=5 s: 9/39 (23%)
```

### The honest reading

**The paper's claim as written is not supported.** "A pass thirty seconds in may
rewrite a word from the first sentence" describes something I did not observe:
the deepest revision reached **0.67 back**, and the longest-standing text revised
had stood **12 s**, not 30.

And the workload cannot exercise it. Utterance durations from the log, n=1502:

```
min 0.3   p25 3.6   median 5.6   p75 8.8   p95 16.7   max 52.3   (seconds)
>=30 s:  9/1502 (0.6%)        >=12 s: 189/1502 (12.6%)
```

A thirty-second-deep revision is possible in **0.6%** of real utterances.

**What is supported, and it is still worth a pattern.** No-frozen-prefix is
genuinely exercised, well beyond "a few words back": a design that froze anything
older than 2 s would corrupt ~23% of revisions, and one that froze past 50% of
the utterance would corrupt 10%. The property earns its keep **at the scale of
seconds**, not thirty seconds. I would publish it that way.

### ⚠️ An unflattering finding I did not go looking for

**Much of the deep-revision mass is oscillation, not convergence.** Eight of the
ten deepest revisions are flip-flop pairs:

```
0.55 back, standing 11 s:  '15'      -> 'fifteen'
0.54 back, standing 12 s:  'fifteen' -> '15'
0.47 back, standing 10 s:  '20,'     -> 'twenty,'
0.46 back, standing 11 s:  'twenty,' -> '20,'
0.51 back, standing  9 s:  'DurEnd'  -> 'Durand'
0.46 back, standing  8 s:  'Durand'  -> 'DurEnd'
```

Number and proper-name formatting churning back and forth between passes. Only
`'here' -> 'hears'` and `'is' -> 'hears'` are unambiguous improvements.

This has a design consequence nobody has looked at yet: the display marks a
corrected word **amber for one refinement cycle**, and James has not yet
evaluated whether that reads well. On this evidence it would flash amber
repeatedly on `15`/`fifteen` while carrying no information. Worth a look before
that highlight is defended.

### Caveats on this measurement

Prepared read, one speaker, three segments — not conversational speech and not
James's own dictation. It measures **the corrector in isolation**; what the user
sees is the settled/volatile composition, which may differ. n is small.

## 3. Do fillers survive Parakeet? — VERIFIED. Yes, completely.

```
said : "Um, so I was thinking, uh, we should probably restart the service."
got  : "Um so I was thinking uh we should probably restart the service."

said : "I think, um, the the corrector is already dropping them."
got  : "I think um the the corrector is already dropping them."

said : "Uh, can you, um, check whether the lamp is on?"
got  : "Uh can you um check whether the lamp is on?"
```

Identical biased and unbiased. The doubled function word (`the the`) survives too
— which is the other thing the house style removes.

**So 0 of 494 is a fact about James, not about the corrector.** He does not say
fillers while holding a push-to-talk chord. The house style's filler rules are
removing something that does not arrive in this workload.

⚠️ **Synthesised, not human.** A synthesiser renders "um" as a clean lexical
token; a human filler is a disfluency with different acoustics. This shows a
*written* filler survives the corrector. It does **not** show that a *spoken*
disfluency would. To settle that properly, record James saying them deliberately.

## 4. The ranking question — two orderings, and the change log misstates one

**Yes, they are two separate orderings in two places, with no shared
representation.** Stating it plainly, as asked.

**Ordering A — live cues.** `resolve()`, `render.rs:517`. An if/else chain over
TTL'd slots:

```
Confirm > Error > Listening > Speaking > Synthesising > Tool > Thinking > Idle
```

**Ordering B — the resting look.** A separate if/return chain inside `frame()`,
reached only under `if mood == Mood::Idle` (`render.rs` ~572–615):

```
presence-off (dark) > scene > presence-on > ambient
```

**And the change log's composite is wrong where it matters.** It records:

```
presence-off (dark) > live cue > scene > presence-on > ambient
```

But ordering B executes **only when no live cue is active**. So in the code a
live cue *pre-empts* the presence-off check entirely — **live cue > presence-off**,
the reverse of what is written.

Whether that is a bug is arguable, and I lean toward the code being right: if a
cue is arriving, something is demonstrably happening, so "nobody is listening" is
stale information. But it is not what the documentation says, and the claim **"the
ranking is the safety model" is materially weaker than it sounds**: the safety
property is split across two structures of different shape (an enum + if/else
chain, and a sequence of early returns), neither of which can see the other, and
no test asserts the composite order. Two people could each change one and keep
their own invariant while breaking the joint one.

That is the weakness stated rather than discovered in review.

## 5. Section J + D — the lighting rules

### J.1 Reassertion, not change detection — TESTIMONY (both incidents), code VERIFIED

**The constraint.** Anything ambient and long-lived must re-assert its state
rather than send on change. The two failure modes are a dropped datagram and a
restarted consumer, and **both are silent** — the producer believes it succeeded.

**The code.** `emit()` in `render.rs`: `REASSERT = 3 s`; when it elapses,
`last_rgb` and `last_device` are cleared, so the next frame re-sends colour and
brightness even though nothing changed. On the tray side, presence is
heartbeated every 5 s.

**Incident A — the lamp came up white instead of violet.**
*Symptom:* James noticed the resting lamp looked "medium" rather than a faint
colour. *Cause:* a power-off → power-on makes a Govee lamp restore **its own**
default; the renderer's colour frame went out microseconds later in the same
tick, and `emit()` sent only on change — so that single fire-and-forget datagram
was the only attempt ever made. A live cue repaints every frame and a lost packet
self-corrects in 66 ms; a **resting** state sends once and stays wrong forever
while the renderer believes it succeeded. *Diagnosis needed* querying the lamp
directly (`lamp status`), which itself only works with the renderer stopped,
because the renderer holds the fixed reply port 4002.

**Incident B — the same bug one layer up, exposed by fixing A.**
*Symptom:* stopping the renderer to inspect it, then restarting it, produced **no
presence at all** — the lamp fell back to ambient and quietly stopped meaning
anything. *Cause:* the tray also announced only on change, so a renderer started
*after* the tray never learns. Fixed by the 5 s heartbeat.

*Time to find:* not recorded numerically; the change-log entry reads as a single
session, with incident B found immediately by the act of diagnosing A.

**How you would know this rule is unnecessary.** If the transport were reliable
and ordered *and* a consumer could ask for current state on connect. Concretely:
a renderer that on startup issued "what is the present state?" would not need the
heartbeat, and a lamp protocol with acknowledgements would not need `REASSERT`.
The rule is a consequence of fire-and-forget UDP to a device that can power-cycle
underneath you, plus consumers that can restart independently.

---

### J.2 Presence means "reachable", not "mode selected" — TESTIMONY

**The constraint.** A new indicator inherits responsibility for everything it
appears to assert.

**The code.** The tray probes `agent::is_available` every 10 s **on its own
thread**. That detail is load-bearing: `is_available` has a 2 s timeout and the
tray loop is the message pump that draws the icon and the overlay, so probing
inline would freeze the interface for two seconds per poll whenever the channel
accepted a connection and then hung. The probe now runs **unconditionally** —
previously it ran only in Agent mode, and when modes were removed it would
otherwise never have run again, leaving the lamp asserting something never
checked.

**The incident.** A dead Vera — service restarted, port lost, tmux session gone —
looked identical to a live one until you had spoken an entire sentence into
nothing. *Symptom:* you talk, and nothing happens. While the tray was silent this
was a **gap**; the moment the lamp began asserting a confident resting glow that
said she was listening, the same gap became a **lie**. Verified in both
directions: `VOICE_AGENT_ENDPOINT=http://127.0.0.1:9999` → "nobody is listening";
real endpoint → "vera is listening".

**How you would know this rule is unnecessary.** If failure were immediate and
visible at the moment of acting — if holding the chord against a dead channel
failed loudly, at once, before you had committed a sentence. The indicator exists
because push-to-talk gives no feedback until after you have already spoken, so
the cost of discovering unreachability is a whole utterance. On a surface where
the attempt itself reports failure synchronously, the ambient indicator is
redundant.

### J.3 A name, never a value — protocol-level, not validation — code VERIFIED, incident TESTIMONY

**The constraint.** An agent may name a scene from a closed vocabulary
(`focus calm warm cool bright dim evening`). It cannot express RGB, brightness or
a waveform. The renderer supplies pigment and dynamics, exactly as it does for
presence.

**Why this is protocol-level and not validation, which is the whole point.** A
validator is a check someone has to remember to write, can be bypassed by a
second code path, and drifts when duplicated. A vocabulary with **no field for a
waveform cannot carry one**. So "never allow agent-generated strobing" stops
being a rule anyone enforces and becomes a fact about what is expressible. The
tray deliberately does **not** validate the name as it relays — the renderer is
the single choke point at the hardware, and a second copy of the policy would
drift from the first.

**The code.** `Scene::lookup(&name)` returns `Option<&'static Scene>`; unknown
names are refused and the known list is logged, never approximated. Scenes are
static by construction — no waveform — and are re-asserted on the same `REASSERT`
timer as every other resting state.

**Incident (D).** The relay shipped **parsing the wrong key**: the tray matched
`type == "scene"` while the contract, written by the same session, specifies
`{"t":"scene", …}`. *Symptom:* none whatsoever. The line would have arrived,
matched nothing, fallen into the catch-all and done nothing — no error, no log,
no lamp. *Found by:* code review, not by test. The tray→renderer wire is pinned
byte-for-byte by tests; the channel→tray wire is a different boundary and had
none, and **a pass-through event is precisely the kind that cannot report its own
absence**.

Hardened alongside: the renderer treats `off|none|clear|normal|default` as clears,
because the agent side hands the model the word "off" and maps it — and if that
mapping were ever missed, a literal "off" would be refused as an unknown scene
while both halves believed they had agreed.

**How you would know this rule is unnecessary.** If the actuator were incapable
of harm — a display that physically cannot strobe — or if agent output were
reviewed by a human before it reached the hardware. The rule exists because the
actuator is in the room, the agent is unsupervised, and the failure mode
(photosensitive response) is not recoverable by noticing.

### J.4 Manual override, and the at-rest restriction — code VERIFIED, behaviour NOT VERIFIED

**The constraint.** A person always wins — but only where "wins" is decidable.

**The code.** `devStatus` is polled every 20 s (`main.rs:645–654`) and compared
against what the renderer last commanded. A mismatch drops the current scene and
refuses new ones for `OVERRIDE_COOLDOWN` (30 min). The comparison is gated on
`believed_resting_state()`, which **returns `None` unless `self.current ==
Mood::Idle`** (`render.rs:401–402`).

**Why at-rest only, and it is not a shortcut.** A live cue repaints every frame.
Comparing observed device state against a moving target would register a mismatch
almost immediately, so agent scene control would be suspended permanently within
seconds of the first conversation. The restriction is what makes the feature
possible at all.

**Scope, deliberately narrow.** The suspension covers **scenes only**. Dimming
the lamp by hand is a preference about the room; it is not a wish to stop being
told that Vera is unreachable. So a hand on the lamp silences taste and leaves
the instrument alone.

⚠️ **This is the weakest-evidenced rule here.** It is unit-tested and has **never
been confirmed against a real hand on the Govee app** — the change log says so
explicitly and it is still outstanding. Everything above is what the code does,
not what has been observed to happen.

**How you would know this rule is unnecessary.** If the renderer were the only
controller of the device — no vendor app, no schedules, no physical switch. The
rule exists because the floor lamp was observed changing state on its own
mid-session (a schedule, or a hand on the app), which is also why a
capture-and-restore design was rejected: it would have fought whatever else was
driving it.

## 6. The unresolved multi-lamp tension — open question, not resolved

We have not built this. One renderer drives one lamp (desk, `192.168.0.42`). The
floor lamp (`.41`) is deliberately not driven. Here is what I think breaks first,
in order.

**1. Presence has no addressee.** `{"t":"presence","on":true,"agent":"vera"}` says
*who* is present, not *where*. With two lamps you must decide whether presence is
a property of the room or of a surface, and there is no place to put that.
`presence.toml` maps agent → colour; an agent → lamp mapping would put topology
into the protocol, which is the thing "a name, never a value" exists to keep out.

**2. The ranking is per-renderer, not per-room.** Two renderers resolving
independently can disagree — lamp A holding a scene while lamp B shows
presence-off — and the room then asserts two contradictory things at once. This
breaks darkness-as-alarm specifically: that signal depends on **the room** going
dark. One dark lamp beside one lit lamp is not an alarm; it reads as a lamp that
broke.

**3. Manual override becomes ambiguous.** A hand on lamp A should presumably
suspend agent control of lamp A. Should it suspend lamp B? If yes, one touch
silences an instrument across the room. If no, the room half-obeys and the
"person always wins" rule is no longer true of the room, only of a bulb.

**4. Port 4002 is a single resource.** Replies always arrive on that fixed port
whatever port the request left from — `lamp status` already cannot run while the
renderer holds it. Two renderers on one host would contend for it directly.

**My guess at the least-bad shape**, offered as a guess: one renderer driving
many lamps, with lamps carrying **roles** (instrument vs ambience) rather than one
renderer per lamp. That keeps the ranking single, keeps the room speaking with
one voice, and gives presence somewhere to land — the instrument role — without
the protocol learning about topology. Untested, and I would not publish it as
more than a hypothesis.
